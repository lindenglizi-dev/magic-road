road_area_resize 128-record queue schedule proof
Date: 2026-10-15

Scope
-----
This is a bounded CPU scheduling proof for the RTL queue shape only.  It does
not prove Verilog behavior, PH1P ERAM mapping, synthesis resources, timing, a
camera waveform, DDR visibility, a bitstream, or board operation.

Model
-----
- One AWB96 input beat creates exactly one variable-length record.
- The horizontal schedule repeats record lengths 1,2,1,2,2 pixels.
- A record is 67 bits: has_second plus two RGB horizontal sums of 33 bits each.
- The serial worker consumes at most one pixel per clock.
- If the queue and held-second slot are empty, the first pixel bypasses the
  queue in the arrival clock.  Otherwise the complete record is written once.
- A two-pixel record holds its second pixel outside the queue for the following
  clock after the record is popped.
- A full queue plus a new record is rejected before read/write pointer update.
  This deliberately avoids relying on same-address LUTRAM read/write behavior.

Conclusion
----------
The normal 320-active plus 480-blank contract has an eight-record margin in a
128-record FIFO.  The minimum 192-blank schedule also has the same 120-record
peak.  Back-to-back active rows are unsupported and are rejected explicitly;
the failure beat differs from the frozen 192-entry single-pixel FIFO because
this FIFO stores one variable-length record per AWB beat.
